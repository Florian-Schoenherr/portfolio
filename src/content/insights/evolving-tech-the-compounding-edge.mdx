Florian wrote the cautious post, and he is right about the costs. I am not
going to pretend they don't exist. What I want to push back on is the
implicit conclusion: that *because* every migration has a price, the
default should be to stand still. In 2026, standing still is not free.
It is the most expensive thing a small engineering team can do.

The price of standing still used to be zero. Your stack worked, the
industry moved at human speed, and a competent team could quietly run a
boring system for a decade. That world is gone. The cost of compute, of
shipping, of automating a once-manual task, has fallen so fast that a
team using last year's tools is competing with a team that gets the same
work done in two thirds of the time. The gap shows up not in a single
quarter, but in the third one, when their estimates are smaller than
yours and the client notices.

The opportunities column is where compounding lives. A 10% improvement
on a single task is forgettable. The same 10% across every PR review,
every CI run, every onboarding, for four quarters in a row, is what
separates a team that grows from one that survives. Compounded gains are
*invisible per week and decisive per year* — which is why teams that
optimise for the week always lose to teams that optimise for the year.

My rule is the inverse of Florian's, but it lands in the same place: adopt
on evidence, not on hype, and only when the team can name *what they
will stop doing* to make room. Otherwise it is just another tab in the
browser. But when the evidence is there, move — and don't apologise for
it.
