The cautious assessment of migration costs is a good idea, just like Florian wrote; those expenses are real and cannot be ignored. However, the implicit conclusion—that because every migration has a price, standing still should be the default choice—is flawed. In 2026, maintaining the status quo is no longer free. For a small engineering team, it can become the most expensive decision available.

Historically, the cost of standing still was near zero. Technology stacks remained stable, the industry evolved at a manageable pace, and a competent team could run a legacy system reliably for a decade. That environment no longer exists. The costs of compute, deployment, and automation have dropped so rapidly that a team relying on yesterday's tooling now competes against teams that complete the same workload in two-thirds of the time. This efficiency gap rarely surfaces in the first quarter; it becomes visible by the third quarter, when competing estimates are consistently lower and clients notice the difference.

Opportunities in engineering are about compounding gains. A 10% improvement on a single, isolated task has a negligible impact. However, when that same 10% efficiency gain is applied across every PR review, every CI run, and every onboarding process, the cumulative effect is significant.
Over four quarters, these marginal gains separate engineering teams that scale from teams that merely survive. Compounded progress is invisible on a weekly timeline, but it is decisive on an annual timeline. Consequently, teams that optimize for short-term fixes consistently lose to teams that build for long-term efficiency.

Technology adoption should be driven by measurable evidence rather than industry trends. To maintain operational capacity, a team should only introduce a new tool if they can explicitly identify an existing process or tool they will stop using to make room.
Without this strict trade-off, adding new technology only increases cognitive load and operational complexity. However, when clear data proves that a new tool creates a distinct advantage, the team should execute the migration immediately.
