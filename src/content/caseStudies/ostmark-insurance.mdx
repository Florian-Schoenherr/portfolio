import StarSection from '../../components/StarSection.astro';

<StarSection label="Situation">
  Ostmark Insurance processed around 400 claims per week, every one of them
  arriving as a scanned PDF or fax. Two full-time clerks re-typed each claim
  into the core insurance system. Between the queue, the re-typing, and the
  verification loop, a straightforward claim routinely took seven working days
  to acknowledge — well behind what competitors were offering online.
</StarSection>

<StarSection label="Task">
  Build a claim-intake layer that extracts structured data from the scanned
  forms, validates it against policy and customer data, and routes low-risk
  claims straight into the existing core system — without requiring Ostmark
  to replace that core system or change their broker workflow.
</StarSection>

<StarSection label="Action">
  - Catalogued the 11 in-use claim-form variants and built a labelled dataset
    of 1,200 redacted samples.
  - Prototyped three OCR pipelines and benchmarked them; picked a Tesseract
    + LayoutLM combination with a confidence-gated hand-off to a human
    reviewer for low-confidence fields.
  - Wrapped the pipeline as a FastAPI microservice with a queue-backed
    worker, deployed in Docker on Ostmark's existing on-prem stack.
  - Added a validation service that cross-checks extracted fields against
    the policy database and flags impossible combinations (policy expired,
    claim predates coverage, etc.).
  - Built a lightweight review UI for the two clerks, who pivoted from
    typists to reviewers of exceptions only.
  - Delivered a full CI/CD pipeline with contract tests and a staging
    environment the compliance team could audit.
</StarSection>

<StarSection label="Result">
  Average claim cycle time dropped from **7 days to ~2 days**, a **70%**
  reduction. **63%** of claims now pass through to the core system without
  human touch on the first attempt, and data-entry errors found downstream
  fell by **22%**. The two clerks were redeployed into fraud review, where
  Ostmark had a long-standing backlog.
</StarSection>
